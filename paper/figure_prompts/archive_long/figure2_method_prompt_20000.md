# FIGURE 2 — ACOUSTIC-TO-KV CORRECTION REGRESSION

## 01. Deliverable and scientific message
Create one publication-ready method architecture figure for Acoustic-to-KV Correction Regression, abbreviated AKR, in a machine-learning research paper on animal-sound recognition by speech language models. Produce a single integrated 16:9 landscape composition, not separate competing designs. Its central message is: learn a correction predictor, not a replacement vocabulary or classifier. Labelled support recordings teach a compact predictor how frozen acoustic features map to internal K/V increments. A new recording supplies the features that predict its increment; the original frozen model then generates the recognition label. These instructions are the design brief, not text to print on the image. The finished figure must be understood from its topology, modules, and a few concise equations.

## 02. Canvas, proportions, and reserved space
Use a 1600 by 900 design coordinate system, preserving exactly 16:9 when exported. Keep approximately 55 units of clear margin on the left and right and 40 units above and below the content. The upper band occupies roughly y=65 to 395; the lower band occupies y=485 to 855. Reserve the intervening horizontal gutter for the compact fitted-object bridge and a quiet stage divider. The lower band may be slightly taller because the attention mechanism requires an inset. Place the attention inset in the lower-right region without shrinking the main inference path into tiny boxes. These coordinates specify compositional proportions, not pixel-perfect requirements; move modules to preserve legibility and the causal order. Never solve a collision by making the entire figure's labels smaller.

## 03. Visual language and typography
Use a pure white background, crisp vector strokes, restrained corner rounding, generous internal padding, and aligned rectangular modules. The style should resemble a strong transformer architecture figure rather than a business-process chart. Use one neutral sans-serif typeface for labels and a compatible mathematical typeface for equations. At final manuscript width, ordinary labels should remain approximately 8–9 pt and stage headings approximately 10–11 pt. Module names should usually contain two to four words. Avoid all-capital paragraphs, heavy drop shadows, glass effects, isometric towers, photorealistic animals, decorative microphones, and rounded speech-bubble clouds surrounding every operation. Keep the diagram's visible prose short even though this specification is detailed.

## 04. Color semantics and grayscale readability
Use muted sage for audio features and their forward path, pale lavender for gradient coordinates and predicted corrections, and neutral gray for the unchanged speech-model backbone. A restrained warm ochre accent may identify support-only supervision. Suggested fills are sage #EDF4EF, lavender #F0EDFA, and gray #F4F5F6; use dark charcoal #25313B for text and outlines. The predictor and plus nodes may use slightly stronger outlines to receive attention. Never use color alone to encode frozen versus fitted objects, audio versus text positions, or forward versus supervision flow: also use explicit labels, token brackets, and line styles. All arrows and text must remain readable when printed in grayscale. Do not make positive and negative coefficient signs depend only on red and green.

## 05. Support input cards
At the upper-left, draw a small stack of waveform cards labelled '(x_i, y_i)' with a separate task-prompt chip 'p'. Use only three visible cards plus an ellipsis to represent a general support set; the diagram must not imply a particular number of clips or a balanced number per class. Each card may contain a simple schematic waveform and a short label token, but no empirical spectrogram or measured acoustic contour is required. The waveform represents an observed recording. The label y_i is supplied supervision, not a pseudo-label sampled by the model. Split the support information cleanly: x_i and p go toward feature extraction and the answer-loss computation; y_i goes to the support answer-loss branch only. The label must never appear as an input to the acoustic feature regressor.

## 06. Frozen acoustic-feature path
Draw a feature path labelled 'Frozen feature pass'. It receives the recording and task prompt, processes them through the unchanged speech model, and reads a selected hidden representation at audio-token positions. Depict a short token ribbon with a bracket over the audio positions, followed by a pooling symbol that produces h_i. This feature is not the output of a new encoder trained by AKR, and the label is not appended to its input. A thin forward arrow sends h_i to 'Support standardization', yielding z_i. Show the transformation z_i=(h_i-mu)/sigma in a small mathematical annotation, with division understood featurewise. The statistics mu and sigma are fitted from support features and later reused. The feature layer and the intervention layers are conceptually distinct; do not imply that they must coincide.

## 07. Support answer-loss path
Draw a separate support-only branch through the same frozen model, using the correct class-name tokens to compute answer-token cross-entropy. A concise loss card may show L_ans=-mean_j log p_theta(y_{i,j}|x_i,p,y_{i,<j}). The teacher-forced preceding answer tokens belong only to this loss evaluation. Mask prompt tokens out of the loss. Show the target label entering the loss card through a short supervision connector, not entering the low-rank basis or final inference label directly. From the loss, draw a support-only backward connector toward the selected K/V projection outputs at audio-token positions. Its endpoint must be an activation-state bracket, not a weight matrix. Backpropagation obtains a derivative; no optimizer updates the model parameters theta. A small lock on the backbone remains visible beside this branch.

## 08. Negative state gradients and pooling
At the tapped audio positions, represent per-token state gradients with several small signed arrows or vector cells. Pool these gradients over audio positions within each selected layer/projection block, then concatenate the pooled blocks to obtain g_i. If space allows one definition here, use g_i=concat_{l,s}[-mean_{t in A_i} dL_ans/dZ^s_{i,l,t}], with s in {K,V}. This is the main pooled method: the target is fixed-width even when recordings have different audio-token counts. Do not draw the token-preserving or factorized variant as if it were the main algorithm. Gradients may flow through downstream computations, but the retained targets include only the selected audio-position K/V states. No text-position or answer-position gradient is concatenated into the displayed target vector. Use the short module label 'Pool state gradients'.

## 09. Centering and the low-rank coordinate system
Send the pooled support targets into a module labelled 'Center + SVD'. Represent the stack of targets as a compact rectangular matrix G, then show it decomposing into a mean vector g_bar and a narrow basis B. The mathematical construction is G_c=G-1 g_bar^T=U Sigma V^T, with B formed from the first r right singular vectors. A short outgoing coefficient ribbon is labelled a_i=B^T(g_i-g_bar). The mean must be preserved because the query reconstruction adds it back. The basis describes variation of correction targets, not axes onto which the original K/V activations are projected. Avoid a drawing that pushes existing K or V through B and deletes their other components. B belongs to the correction branch only. Treat rank r as a compact coordinate dimension, not the number of recognition classes.

## 10. Ridge fitting and the second objective
Join standardized support features z_i and target coordinates a_i at a small, clearly distinguished 'Ridge fit' module. Make z_i the predictor input and a_i the supervised target; they are not interchangeable arrows. Place the regression objective beside this module: L_reg=sum_i ||W z_i+b-a_i||_2^2+lambda ||W||_F^2. The penalty applies to W, not to the intercept b. A concise output label 'W, b' identifies the fitted affine map. This squared-error objective is separate from the answer-token cross-entropy used to extract gradient targets. The output coordinates are real-valued signed numbers, not a softmax over animal classes. Do not put a class-probability simplex, top-1 selection, or argmax between this module and the correction basis. Mark this compact module 'Fit' rather than placing a training flame on the backbone.

## 11. Compact fitted-object bridge
Across the stage gutter, place one narrow capsule labelled 'Saved correction predictor'. Its contents are mu, sigma; W, b; B, g_bar. Arrange these as a few small chips, grouping feature statistics, regression coefficients, and correction basis objects. Pass this capsule downward using a clearly identified saved-object connector. No support recording, support answer sequence, per-example gradient bank, or class-centroid dictionary should cross into the query lane. Configuration items such as selected layers and correction strength may be mentioned in the brief but should not be portrayed as values learned from query labels. The reusable basis and normalizer are part of the fitted predictor even though ridge only optimizes W and b. The bridge must visually distinguish reusable fitted objects from a signal backpropagated on each new query.

## 12. Query input and first pass
At the lower-left, draw one new waveform card labelled 'Unlabelled query x_q' and a separate prompt chip p. Do not draw a y_q card, even crossed out, because it creates a misleading visual dependency. The query first enters a box labelled 'Frozen feature pass', with a tiny '1' pass badge. Read h_q from its audio-token representation and apply the saved support normalizer to obtain z_q. The first pass supplies features for correction prediction; it does not claim to have selected the final recognition label. Route the same raw recording and prompt along a lower bypass toward the generation pass. This bypass carries input data, not cached activations retroactively modified using information from the end of the same computation. Leave enough space to distinguish both forward passes.

## 13. Continuous coefficients for the new recording
Send z_q into the saved affine regressor, labelled 'Predict coordinates'. Show the output as a short ribbon a_hat_q=W z_q+b with signed cells. A four-cell illustration is acceptable only as a schematic of a compact vector; label its width r rather than suggesting four classes. The predictor is already fitted at this stage, so use an 'Apply' badge or no badge, not a second training symbol. There is no query loss, gradient extraction, label lookup, or hyperparameter search in this lane. The predictor does not select a support example or retrieve one class's correction. Its coefficients continuously combine the shared basis directions. Different waveforms can therefore produce different combinations even when a later decoder happens to emit the same label. Do not show numerical coefficient values unless labelled purely schematic.

## 14. Correction reconstruction as the visual centerpiece
Make the reconstruction block the most prominent compact module in the lower lane. Its equation is g_hat_q=g_bar+B(W z_q+b), or equivalently g_hat_q=g_bar+B a_hat_q if the affine map was already shown immediately upstream. Give this equation slightly more whitespace and stronger visual weight than other formulas. Use a small matrix-times-vector glyph to show that B expands the low-dimensional coefficients into a full correction vector. Then use an 'Unpack + scale' operation to produce the selected Delta K and Delta V blocks. The additions are scaled by a fixed operating strength eta in the main schematic. Do not introduce a learned gating network, adaptive step controller, iterative optimizer, or new correction head beyond the stated ridge map. Keep the original states outside this reconstruction block.

## 15. Second forward pass and native answer
The bypassed x_q and p enter a second frozen-model box, with a tiny '2' pass badge and the label 'Corrected generation'. Predicted K/V increments arrive through a side port at selected attention blocks. The model processes the recording with those additive changes and then uses its original language-model head and vocabulary to produce text. At the far right, use one modest output card labelled 'Predicted label', containing y_hat_q or an illustrative class string such as 'Trill'. Do not mark it with a guaranteed-correct checkmark. Show the native head as part of the same frozen model, not an external ridge classifier. The regressor's output must connect to internal states, never directly to the final label card. The original recognition prompt still defines the task and available label conventions.

## 16. Attention inset: exact insertion sites
Include one enlarged attention inset connected by a thin zoom leader to the corrected-generation model. Begin with hidden activations H_l feeding q_proj, k_proj, and v_proj. On the K branch, place a plus node immediately after k_proj at its projection output Z^K; add the predicted Delta K there, before head reshaping and before rotary position encoding. On the V branch, place a plus node immediately after v_proj at Z^V, before head reshaping; add Delta V there. RoPE belongs on Q and K only, not V. Preserve the unmodified original branch as an input to each plus node. The updated K and V continue into ordinary attention and the resulting cache representation. This is not a diagram of overwriting already rotated keys with unrotated gradients. Label the inset 'Audio-position state addition'. Only direct additions are masked; downstream text and Q states can also change.

## 17. Audio mask and pooled broadcast
Beside the inset, show one short strip containing neutral prompt-token cells, a bracketed group of sage audio-token cells, and a final neutral response region. Indicate the active audio mask with a bracket A_q rather than printing a long binary vector. Fan a single K/V block increment to multiple audio positions to convey broadcast. The same pooled vector is reused across the audio positions of a given recording and block; it is not a different learned vector for every token. Another recording would produce another block vector. Keep text and answer positions outside the direct-addition bracket. If the strip becomes crowded, show the mask once and remove duplicate token strips elsewhere. A schematic token count must never be confused with the true number of expanded audio tokens or a fixed recording length.

## 18. Arrows and connection grammar
Use solid arrows for ordinary forward data, warm dashed arrows for support-only supervised dependencies, and lavender dashed arrows for transfer of fitted objects. Limit the legend to 'Forward', 'Support only', and 'Saved objects'. A dashed connector in the lower lane denotes a saved parameter input, never a query backward pass. Put arrowheads at actual receiving ports and avoid diagonal lines passing across unrelated modules. For branches, use a small junction dot only where a shared signal genuinely splits; line crossings without data exchange should have no junction dot. Prefer orthogonal routing with consistent bend radii. The only backward-directed computational connector is confined to the support loss-to-state-gradient route. Keep the transport of fitted objects visually distinct from the loss-gradient route even if both are dashed.

## 19. Shape consistency for implementation-aware drawing
For internal checking, n is the support size, d the feature width, P the concatenated correction width, and r the retained rank. G has shape n by P, B has shape P by r, W has shape r by d, and b and a_i have length r. Both g_i and g_hat_q have length P. The normalizer has feature width d. At a selected layer and projection, unpacking yields the width of that K or V projection output, which need not equal the entire hidden width in every architecture. Represent these shape relationships through narrow or wide glyphs, not through literal thousands of cells. At most print d, r, and P on three representative edges. The figure should remain generic about actual layer counts and feature indices, because those choices belong to the declared experimental protocol.

Use the following label vocabulary as a guide: 'Support fitting', 'Labelled recordings', 'Frozen feature pass', 'Answer loss', 'Pool state gradients', 'Center + SVD', 'Ridge fit', 'Saved correction predictor', 'Query inference', 'Unlabelled query', 'Predict coordinates', 'Reconstruct correction', 'Unpack + scale', 'Corrected generation', 'Original LM head', and 'Predicted label'. Keep the total visible prose near 120–170 words, excluding equations and symbols.

## 21. What the architecture must not imply
Do not invent a teacher model, an animal-sound generator, a second vocabulary, a reward model, a policy-distillation objective, reinforcement learning, or iterative test-time training. Do not insert a class router before the correction basis: class-routed gradients are a different variant. Do not represent AKR as LoRA, because its additions are to inference states rather than learned weight matrices. Do not show a one-size-fits-all global direction as the full method; retain the mean plus input-conditioned term. Do not label the procedure entirely training-free, since it includes supervised support fitting. Do not show a low-pass filter as a module that AKR reverses or reconstructs: the method predicts state increments, not missing high-frequency waveforms. No extension result or ablation may replace the stated pooled continuous-regression pipeline.

## 22. Caption and separation of detail
Use an external manuscript caption, not a title banner inside the image. Its intended wording is: 'Learn a correction predictor, not a replacement vocabulary. Labelled support supplies audio-position K/V gradient targets and label-free acoustic features for a low-rank regressor. Saved predictor objects map each new recording to additive state increments before the original frozen model generates text. Dashed paths denote support supervision or fitted-object transfer, not query backpropagation.' Keep precise sample counts, support-selection rules, feature-layer indices, eta versus relative-alpha settings, and full loss definitions in the manuscript or appendix. Do not turn these settings into tiny footnotes inside the artwork. This figure communicates the algorithm's dependencies, not an experimental leaderboard or a theorem that all predicted updates improve accuracy.
